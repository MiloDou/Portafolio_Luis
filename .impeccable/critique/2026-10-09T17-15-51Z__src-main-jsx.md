The top-level promise spans design, social media, illustration, and digital content, while the work gallery currently shows only two illustrations.

## Overall Impression

The portfolio is energetic and memorable, and its package section now gives prospective clients concrete prices and deliverables. The biggest opportunity is to make the work and contact path as credible and complete as the visual identity.

## What's Working

- The face illustration and high-contrast palette make the first screen recognizable.
- “Sobre mí,” “Trabajos,” and “Hablemos” match the visitor's broad goals.
- Package tabs group four priced offers, with deliverables and service conditions available on demand.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] The public profile still looks unfinished.** “Tu ciudad,” “Añade tu correo,” “Añade WhatsApp,” and “Añade tu Behance” appear in the experience. “Escríbeme” falls back to the contact anchor because no email is set. This weakens trust at the moment a potential client wants to reach Luis. Replace placeholders with verified details or remove empty rows; make the CTA call or message the available contact method. **Suggested command:** $impeccable clarify.

2. **[P1] The work gallery does not support the breadth of the service promise.** Two illustration pieces do not yet prove capability across illustration, social content, graphic design, and digital content. Add selected social/design work from Canva as visible previews or case studies, with Luis's role and a concise brief; if that work is not ready, narrow the positioning to what the gallery proves. **Suggested command:** $impeccable onboard.

3. **[P2] “VER PIEZA” is a false affordance.** It is rendered as a visual label inside a non-interactive article, so keyboard and pointer users cannot open the work. Link each card to a detail view or larger image, or remove the action treatment. **Suggested command:** $impeccable harden.

4. **[P2] The hero headline competes with the face illustration.** On desktop, “con carácter” crosses the artwork's focal area, while large section headings can dominate the work itself. Keep the same expressive type but constrain its position and size so the art remains legible. **Suggested command:** $impeccable layout.

5. **[P2] Small labels and metadata are difficult to scan.** Several captions and package labels use 9–11 px type, with some low contrast. Increase the smallest text and strengthen contrast while preserving the visual hierarchy. **Suggested command:** $impeccable typeset.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-time visitor:** Sees “Tu ciudad” and empty contact prompts, then must infer whether the main contact action will reach Luis. The page should show only usable contact methods and a clear next step.
- **Riley, deliberate evaluator:** Finds two illustration examples beneath a broad multi-service offer; there is not enough visible case evidence to judge fit for social-media work.
- **Casey, mobile visitor:** May tap “VER PIEZA” or the “01 / 02” indicator expecting an interactive control. The portfolio should align visible affordances with working actions.

## Cognitive Load

Moderate: three checklist failures—single focus (the hero headline overlaps the illustration), chunking (package details form a long six-item list), and working memory (comparing one-at-a-time package tabs requires remembering the previous plan). The four package choices remain within the checklist's four-option threshold, and service conditions use progressive disclosure.

## Minor Observations

- “2023 — EN ADELANTE” is less useful than a current update date or project timeframe.
- With two gallery items, the “Todo / Ilustración” filter adds little value.
- The hero counter looks like a gallery control even though it is static.
- A package comparison summary could make quantities, duration, revisions, publishing, and account management easier to compare.

## Questions to Consider

- Which service should a new visitor remember first: illustration, social content, or graphic design?
- Would visible Canva previews make the social-media offer credible without competing with the illustrated portfolio?
- Should the hero headline remain oversized if it obscures part of the featured illustration?
